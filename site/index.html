Model v. bookmakers

How much of a Champions League knockout is luck?

Before the first leg of every knockout tie, a model worked out each team's chance of going through, using only matches played earlier. We call it the pre-match chance. Then we checked what actually happened.

Winning it all takes four or five wins in a row

To lift the trophy, a champion has to win every tie on its way. Multiply its pre-match chances together and you get the odds of that whole run. Any club needing that many wins gets low odds, so a low number doesn't mean the champion was weak. Hover or tap a bar, or pick a champion below, to see its route.

      Luck, or a blind spot? Real Madrid

      How many ties each club won, against how many the model expected it to win (clubs with at least 8 ties). Most clubs sit close to what the model expected. One doesn't.

      Real Madrid went through ties when the model expected . If the model were right, that would happen about once in tries. That is for Real Madrid on its own. I noticed Real Madrid before testing them, so the fairer question is how often the most unusual of all clubs with at least ties would look this extreme if every tie followed the model. It would still happen only about once in tries. The model rates Real Madrid accurately in La Liga, but in Champions League knockout matches they take about a third of a point a match more than expected.

      So part of Real Madrid's "luck" isn't luck. Either they lift their level for these matches, or something that matters here isn't visible in league results.

      Is the model honest?

      A luck study is only as good as its chances. So the model was tested on league matches from seasons it never saw (2017-18 onwards), against the bookmakers' final odds just before kick-off, which are the toughest benchmark there is.

      When it says 60%, does it happen 60% of the time?

      Home teams are grouped by the chance they were given, then we count how often they actually won. A point on the "honest" line means the chances were right.

      How close does it get to the bookmakers?

      0% is knowing nothing: giving every match the same average home, draw and away chances. 100% is matching the bookmakers.

      The bookmakers win, as expected: their odds include team news, injuries and the betting market's money. The model gets most of the way there using nothing but past results.

      The exact scores
      ModelBrierLog loss

      Both measure how far the chances given were from what happened, averaged over every match; lower is better. Brier counts each miss by its size; log loss punishes confident wrong calls harder, such as saying 90% for a team that then loses.
